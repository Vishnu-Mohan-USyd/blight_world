# Evidence ledger for the perception architecture

## Scope and evidence interpretation

This ledger links computational claims to original studies and records the distinctions that change the architecture. The evidence includes human psychophysics and lesions, human imaging, primate recordings and tracing, and rodent circuit interventions. Cross-species findings constrain candidate mechanisms; they do not establish a complete human circuit simulation.

The synthesis covers major pathways needed for embodied perception, memory-guided interpretation and action. It is not a census of all nuclei, cell types or developmental mechanisms. Publisher text, primary-study abstracts and author-hosted records were consulted; full-text access varied. Abstract-level findings are not extended to uninspected methodological details.

## Cross-comparison decisions

### Visual item limits versus graded precision

Classic change detection emphasizes a limit near four simple objects; continuous report shows precision and priority effects.

**Architectural consequence:** Representational precision, similarity and selection must remain explicit. A universal four-slot container is not warranted.

**Primary evidence:** [Luck and Vogel (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1038/36846); [Bays and Husain (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1158023).

### Shared versus modality-specific working-memory capacity

Masked dual-array tasks reveal shared limits, while minimizing shared codes and coordination yields greater independence.

**Architectural consequence:** Use content-specific retention with shared representational/control bottlenecks; vary task demands rather than average the findings into one number.

**Primary evidence:** [Saults and Cowan (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.136.4.663); [Fougnie and Marois (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024834); [Fougnie et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0038211).

### Duration-based phonological accounts

Original word-length effects linked span to articulation duration; later controlled comparisons do not support a universal duration-only explanation.

**Architectural consequence:** Implement sequence interference and rehearsal explicitly; do not assume a fixed biological two-second expiry timer.

**Primary evidence:** [Baddeley, Thomson and Buchanan (1975)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(75)80045-4); [Lovatt, Avons and Masterson (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1080/713755877).

### Persistent versus latent temporary retention

Some retained content is expressed in ongoing sensory or distributed activity; latent content can later be reactivated. Frontal-thalamic loops causally support persistence.

**Architectural consequence:** Separate stored availability from current expression and task accessibility. One finding does not eliminate the other mechanisms.

**Primary evidence:** [Harrison and Tong (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07832); [Rose et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aah7011); [Guo et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22324); [Mozumder et al. (2026 (online 24 December 2025))](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2025.116764).

### Hippocampus at short delays

Relational short-delay deficits coexist with preserved performance under lower load and particular delay conditions.

**Architectural consequence:** Specify binding, load and interference; do not classify a mechanism by delay length alone.

**Primary evidence:** [Olson et al. (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1923-05.2006); [Jeneson, Mauldin, Hopkins and Squire (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1101/lm.2010711).

### DG separation and CA3 completion

Input and output population measurements plus selective perturbation support different transformations and reduced-cue retrieval.

**Architectural consequence:** Test both interference resistance and partial-cue completion. Completion must not erase episode distinctions.

**Primary evidence:** [Neunuebel and Knierim (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.11.017); [Nakazawa et al. (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1071795).

### CA2 routing and CA1 convergence

CA2 is a parallel input to CA1 with differentiated connectivity; CA1 combines inputs and changes representational structure.

**Architectural consequence:** Preserve parallel routes. Do not insert CA2 in an obligatory numerical chain or assert that CA1 is a literal subtraction operator.

**Primary evidence:** [Kohara et al. (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3614); [Hitti and Siegelbaum (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13028); [Remondes and Schuman (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1038/416736a); [Maimon et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10537-0).

### Entorhinal return route

Deep entorhinal input and output populations differ; human retrieval involves cortical reinstatement.

**Architectural consequence:** Model a transformed reinstatement pathway rather than a single hippocampal output cable. Directed human subfield-to-cortex timing remains less resolved than the broad mechanism.

**Primary evidence:** [Surmeli et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.10.041); [Horner et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms8462); [Tompary, Duncan and Davachi (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1002/hipo.22582).

### Multisensory fusion and source assignment

Common-cause judgments and reliability both influence integration; some results depart from simple optimal weighting.

**Architectural consequence:** Separate assignment from fusion and allow task-dependent departures from optimality.

**Primary evidence:** [Kording et al. (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0000943); [Rohe and Noppeney (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002073); [Ernst and Banks (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1038/415429a); [Fetsch, Turner, DeAngelis and Angelaki (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.2574-09.2009).

### Recurrence has several demonstrated functions

Recurrent processing supports figure-ground organization, concentration invariance and stability against degraded input in different circuits.

**Architectural consequence:** Give recurrent modules operation-specific validation tasks rather than declaring all recurrence to be one prediction-error algorithm.

**Primary evidence:** [Self et al. (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1119527109); [Bolding and Franks (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aat6904); [Bolding et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.53125).

### Contextual networks and task control

Event, social and hidden-state representations are differentiated; control coupling changes with task.

**Architectural consequence:** Maintain separate event/context and task-control functions with task-dependent communication. An atlas network name does not supply the update rule.

**Primary evidence:** [Baldassano et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2017.06.041); [DiNicola, Braga and Buckner (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00529.2019); [Spreng et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2010.06.016); [Schuck et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2016.08.019).

### Selection and thalamic mechanisms

Evidence supports sensory gain changes, inhibitory thalamic selection, and maintenance of cortical task dynamics.

**Architectural consequence:** Distinguish input gain, stability and readout/selection rather than substituting one scalar attention variable.

**Primary evidence:** [Moore and Armstrong (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01341); [Wimmer et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature15398); [Schmitt et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22073); [Guo et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22324).

### Action gating and learning signals

Basal-ganglia routes interact during action; motor prediction errors and reward/motivation signals have different demonstrated roles.

**Architectural consequence:** Use interacting selection mechanisms and error-specific learning targets. Keep movement correction distinct from across-trial adaptation.

**Primary evidence:** [Nambu et al. (2000)](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.2000.84.1.289); [Cui et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11846); [Tseng et al. (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00266.2007); [Schultz, Dayan and Montague (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593); [Mohebi et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1235-y).

### Stress and the operating regime

Internal state changes neural responsiveness and task-related activity; concrete causal pathways exist for specific needs.

**Architectural consequence:** Stress and bodily state should alter processing and control, not only a final reward term. Scenario-specific behavioral consequences need testing.

**Primary evidence:** [Hermans et al. (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1209603); [Qin et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2009.03.006); [Livneh et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22375).

### Functional integration and consciousness

The preregistered comparison challenged specific predictions from both tested theories.

**Architectural consequence:** Validate coherent perception and flexible access directly; do not label the modular implementation conscious on the strength of task performance alone.

**Primary evidence:** [COGITATE Consortium et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08888-1).

## Anatomical and functional edge audit

### Cortical hierarchy: ascending and descending projections

Structured laminar pathways support different directions of influence. **Interpretive boundary:** Exact prediction/error labels are additional hypotheses.

Source: [Markov et al. (2014), Anatomy of hierarchy: feedforward and feedback pathways in macaque visual cortex](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.23458).

### Frontal eye fields -> V4

Stimulation modifies spatially corresponding visual responses. **Interpretive boundary:** Goal-directed gain is supported; a binary attention mask is an implementation.

Source: [Moore and Armstrong (2003), Selective gating of visual signals by microstimulation of frontal cortex](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01341).

### SC -> MD thalamus -> frontal eye fields

Motor-related signal contributes to predictive spatial updating. **Interpretive boundary:** Provides part of the self-motion correction mechanism.

Source: [Sommer and Wurtz (2006), Influence of the thalamus on spatial visual processing in frontal cortex](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature05279).

### Prefrontal-dependent control -> visual TRN/LGN processing

Selection involves inhibitory thalamic gain control. **Interpretive boundary:** Functional dependence is not used to invent an unverified direct monosynaptic edge.

Source: [Wimmer et al. (2015), Thalamic control of sensory selection in divided attention](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature15398).

### MD thalamus <-> prefrontal cortex

Thalamic input supports cortical task representations. **Interpretive boundary:** A change in effective interaction need not change anatomical wiring.

Source: [Schmitt et al. (2017), Thalamic amplification of cortical connectivity sustains attentional control](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22073).

### Frontal cortex <-> motor-related thalamus

Reciprocal activity supports retained preparatory state. **Interpretive boundary:** Retention can be a loop property rather than one local buffer.

Source: [Guo et al. (2017), Maintenance of persistent activity in a frontal thalamocortical loop](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22324).

### EC II -> DG/CA3; DG -> CA3

Convergent input participates in differentiated representations. **Interpretive boundary:** The memory operation depends on input regime and learning.

Source: [Neunuebel and Knierim (2014), CA3 retrieves coherent representations from degraded input: direct evidence for CA3 pattern completion and dentate gyrus pattern separation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.11.017).

### EC II and DG -> CA2 -> CA1

Cell-type-resolved tracing and physiology identify the parallel CA2 route. **Interpretive boundary:** CA2 favors deep CA1; do not infer that CA3 favors superficial CA1 from this experiment.

Source: [Kohara et al. (2014), Cell type-specific genetic and optogenetic tools reveal hippocampal CA2 circuits](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3614).

### CA3 and EC III -> CA1

Convergent inputs influence CA1 spiking and plasticity. **Interpretive boundary:** Convergence is established; exact numerical comparison is not.

Source: [Remondes and Schuman (2002), Direct cortical input modulates plasticity and spiking in CA1 pyramidal neurons](https://doi.org/10.1038/416736a).

### Hippocampus -> deep entorhinal populations -> cortical outputs

Input and output cell populations are differentiated. **Interpretive boundary:** No obligatory universal Vb-to-Va transfer algorithm is asserted.

Source: [Surmeli et al. (2015), Molecularly Defined Circuitry Reveals Input-Output Segregation in Deep Layers of the Medial Entorhinal Cortex](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.10.041).

### Hippocampal retrieval <-> cortical event representations

Retrieval is associated with memory-specific cortical reinstatement. **Interpretive boundary:** Human imaging supports the linked process, not every directed synaptic step.

Source: [Horner et al. (2015), Evidence for holistic episodic recollection via hippocampal pattern completion](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms8462).

### Visual/somatosensory association areas <-> AIP <-> F5

Convergent anatomical routes connect object information with grasp-related circuitry. **Interpretive boundary:** The proposed action interface must still learn its coordinate and control mappings.

Source: [Borra et al. (2008 (online 2007)), Cortical Connections of the Macaque Anterior Intraparietal (AIP) Area](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhm146).

### Cortex -> STN -> pallidal output

A fast excitatory route is demonstrated in primates. **Interpretive boundary:** Decision threshold or global pause is a functional interpretation.

Source: [Nambu et al. (2000), Excitatory cortical inputs to pallidal neurons via the subthalamic nucleus in the monkey](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.2000.84.1.289).

### Motor/PFC -> cerebellar loops -> thalamus/cortex

Tracing identifies differentiated closed-loop relationships. **Interpretive boundary:** One universal cerebellar algorithm is not inferred from shared circuit motifs.

Source: [Kelly and Strick (2003), Cerebellar loops with motor cortex and prefrontal cortex of a nonhuman primate](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.23-23-08432.2003).

### Hypothalamic hunger circuitry -> thalamic/amygdala route -> insular cues

Causal state-dependent modulation of food-cue representations. **Interpretive boundary:** Do not substitute this exact route for every motivation or emotion.

Source: [Livneh et al. (2017), Homeostatic circuits selectively gate food cue responses in insular cortex](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22375).

## Source-by-source provenance

### 1. Markov et al. (2014)

[Anatomy of hierarchy: feedforward and feedback pathways in macaque visual cortex](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.23458). DOI: 10.1002/cne.23458.

**Evidence type:** Macaque quantitative tracing.

**Supports:** Laminar origin and termination constrain cortical hierarchy; both ascending and descending streams have structured projections.

**Boundary:** Anatomy does not identify every message as prediction or error.

### 2. Luck and Vogel (1997)

[The capacity of visual working memory for features and conjunctions](https://doi.org/10.1038/36846). DOI: 10.1038/36846.

**Evidence type:** Human change detection.

**Supports:** Approximately four simple objects under these task conditions; feature conjunctions matter.

**Boundary:** Not a universal count for continuous precision, natural objects, or all modalities.

### 3. Bays and Husain (2008)

[Dynamic shifts of limited working memory resources in human vision](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1158023). DOI: 10.1126/science.1158023.

**Evidence type:** Human continuous-report psychophysics.

**Supports:** Precision depends on set size and priority; resources shift toward saccade targets.

**Boundary:** Resource account competes with discrete-capacity accounts; do not claim final resolution.

### 4. Saults and Cowan (2007)

[A central capacity limit to the simultaneous storage of visual and auditory arrays in working memory](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-3445.136.4.663). DOI: 10.1037/0096-3445.136.4.663.

**Evidence type:** Human dual-task experiments.

**Supports:** After masking, combined auditory/visual estimates did not exceed the larger unimodal capacity, about 3-4 items.

**Boundary:** Task coordination and representational overlap are alternative causes of cross-modal costs.

### 5. Fougnie and Marois (2011)

[What limits working memory capacity? Evidence for modality-specific sources to the simultaneous storage of visual and auditory arrays](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024834). DOI: 10.1037/a0024834.

**Evidence type:** Human behavioral experiments.

**Supports:** Evidence for modality-specific contributions to storage.

**Boundary:** Full text access blocked; use later 2015 abstract for detailed claims.

### 6. Fougnie et al. (2015)

[Working memory storage is intrinsically domain specific](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0038211). DOI: 10.1037/a0038211.

**Evidence type:** Human dual-task experiments.

**Supports:** Visual-spatial and auditory-object tasks could be maintained independently when coordination and content overlap were minimized; shared spatial format reintroduced costs.

**Boundary:** Does not imply all modalities are independent or control is unlimited.

### 7. Sperling (1960)

[The information available in brief visual presentations](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0093759). DOI: 10.1037/h0093759.

**Evidence type:** Human partial report.

**Supports:** Rapidly available visual information exceeds whole-report capacity and loses accessibility over a short interval.

**Boundary:** Report availability is not a direct measure of phenomenal capacity or a literal image buffer.

### 8. Treisman and Rostron (1972)

[Brief auditory storage: a modification of Sperling's paradigm applied to audition](https://doi.org/10.1016/0001-6918(72)90021-2). DOI: 10.1016/0001-6918(72)90021-2.

**Evidence type:** Human auditory partial report.

**Supports:** Brief auditory storage lost in about one second in this paradigm.

**Boundary:** Other auditory trace paradigms give longer durations; no universal echoic lifetime.

### 9. Baddeley, Thomson and Buchanan (1975)

[Word length and the structure of short-term memory](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(75)80045-4). DOI: 10.1016/S0022-5371(75)80045-4.

**Evidence type:** Human verbal memory.

**Supports:** Word length and articulatory suppression affect immediate span.

**Boundary:** Two-second decay/rehearsal interpretation not established uniquely; compare Lovatt and Campoy.

### 10. Lovatt, Avons and Masterson (2000)

[The word-length effect and disyllabic words](https://doi.org/10.1080/713755877). DOI: 10.1080/713755877.

**Evidence type:** Controlled human word-span experiments.

**Supports:** Duration effects did not replicate across controlled disyllabic sets.

**Boundary:** Reject universal two-second hard buffer from word length alone.

### 11. Li et al. (2025)

[Exploring the capacity and executive function of vibrotactile working memory through the frequency sequencing task](https://doi.org/10.1111/nyas.15327). DOI: 10.1111/nyas.15327.

**Evidence type:** Four human vibrotactile experiments.

**Supports:** About three frequency units in this sequencing task, with task-dependent dual-task differences.

**Boundary:** Not three touch objects or a capacity for whole-body proprioception.

### 12. Harrison and Tong (2009)

[Decoding reveals the contents of visual working memory in early visual areas](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07832). DOI: 10.1038/nature07832.

**Evidence type:** Human fMRI decoding.

**Supports:** Remembered orientation decodable in V1-V4 over delay.

**Boundary:** Decoding alone does not establish local autonomous storage.

### 13. Rose et al. (2016)

[Reactivation of latent working memories with transcranial magnetic stimulation](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aah7011). DOI: 10.1126/science.aah7011.

**Evidence type:** Human TMS and multivariate decoding.

**Supports:** Latent but potentially relevant memory could be reactivated and affect behavior.

**Boundary:** Not unique evidence of synaptic storage or absence of all spiking.

### 14. Guo et al. (2017)

[Maintenance of persistent activity in a frontal thalamocortical loop](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22324). DOI: 10.1038/nature22324.

**Evidence type:** Mouse optogenetics and recording.

**Supports:** ALM and thalamic activity mutually sustain delayed motor preparation.

**Boundary:** Rodent motor-preparation circuit, not a full human working-memory model.

### 15. Schmitt et al. (2017)

[Thalamic amplification of cortical connectivity sustains attentional control](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22073). DOI: 10.1038/nature22073.

**Evidence type:** Mouse causal circuit experiments.

**Supports:** MD input enables rule-specific PFC dynamics; general excitation is not equivalent.

**Boundary:** Functional influence changes acutely, not new anatomical wiring.

### 16. Olson et al. (2006)

[Working memory for conjunctions relies on the medial temporal lobe](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1923-05.2006). DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1923-05.2006.

**Evidence type:** Human medial-temporal lesion study.

**Supports:** Short-delay relational/conjunctive memory can be impaired.

**Boundary:** Compare capacity-controlled lesion studies; time alone does not define memory system.

### 17. Romo et al. (1999)

[Neuronal correlates of parametric working memory in the prefrontal cortex](https://doi.org/10.1038/20939). DOI: 10.1038/20939.

**Evidence type:** Macaque vibrotactile delayed comparison.

**Supports:** PFC activity retains information about first stimulus for comparison.

**Boundary:** Not every tactile detail remains in primary sensory cortex.

### 18. Romo et al. (2002)

[Neuronal correlates of decision-making in secondary somatosensory cortex](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn950). DOI: 10.1038/nn950.

**Evidence type:** Macaque S2 recording.

**Supports:** S2 responses can combine remembered and current vibration information and correlate with choice.

**Boundary:** Correlational circuit contribution.

### 19. Kohara et al. (2014)

[Cell type-specific genetic and optogenetic tools reveal hippocampal CA2 circuits](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3614). DOI: 10.1038/nn.3614.

**Evidence type:** Mouse genetic tracing, optogenetics and slices.

**Supports:** DG to CA2 to deep CA1 provides alternative route; ECIII to CA2 was not observed.

**Boundary:** Mouse cell-type-specific tracing. CA2 preferentially innervated deep CA1; CA3 inputs were of similar strength in deep and superficial CA1 in the cited experiment. Do not impose an obligatory CA3-to-CA2-to-CA1 sequence.

### 20. Hitti and Siegelbaum (2014)

[The hippocampal CA2 region is essential for social memory](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13028). DOI: 10.1038/nature13028.

**Evidence type:** Mouse selective CA2 silencing.

**Supports:** CA2 required for tested social recognition memory.

**Boundary:** Do not generalize to all human social cognition or all memory.

### 21. Neunuebel and Knierim (2014)

[CA3 retrieves coherent representations from degraded input: direct evidence for CA3 pattern completion and dentate gyrus pattern separation](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.11.017). DOI: 10.1016/j.neuron.2013.11.017.

**Evidence type:** Simultaneous rat DG/CA3 recordings.

**Supports:** DG and CA3 transformations differ under cue conflict.

**Boundary:** CA3 can separate or complete depending on input regime; no fixed decoder label.

### 22. Nakazawa et al. (2002)

[Requirement for hippocampal CA3 NMDA receptors in associative memory recall](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1071795). DOI: 10.1126/science.1071795.

**Evidence type:** Mouse selective NMDA-receptor manipulation.

**Supports:** CA3 plasticity important for recall from reduced cues.

**Boundary:** Does not by itself prove all proposed attractor dynamics.

### 23. Remondes and Schuman (2002)

[Direct cortical input modulates plasticity and spiking in CA1 pyramidal neurons](https://doi.org/10.1038/416736a). DOI: 10.1038/416736a.

**Evidence type:** Rat hippocampal physiology.

**Supports:** Direct ECIII input interacts with CA1 output/plasticity.

**Boundary:** CA1 should not be implemented merely as a relay or literal scalar subtractor.

### 24. Maimon et al. (2026)

[Sparse-to-dense coding transformation between hippocampal areas CA3 and CA1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10537-0). DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-10537-0.

**Evidence type:** Bat CA3/CA1 recordings in large environments plus model.

**Supports:** Ultrasparse CA3 and denser CA1 spatial codes; trajectory history effects; model explores learning benefits.

**Boundary:** Observed coding transform is data; compression/learning benefit depends on model; species and task limited.

### 25. Mozumder et al. (2026 (online 24 December 2025))

[Asynchronous firing and off states in working memory maintenance](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2025.116764). DOI: 10.1016/j.celrep.2025.116764.

**Evidence type:** Two male macaques, dense PFC/PPC recording.

**Supports:** Distributed persistent population activity and stochastic off periods; coordinated fluctuations reduce fidelity.

**Boundary:** Population persistence in this task does not exclude latent retention or synaptic contributions in other tasks.

### 26. Salzman, Britten and Newsome (1990)

[Cortical microstimulation influences perceptual judgements of motion direction](https://doi.org/10.1038/346174a0). DOI: 10.1038/346174a0.

**Evidence type:** Macaque MT microstimulation and motion judgments.

**Supports:** Direction-selective population stimulation biased judgments toward the stimulated preference.

**Boundary:** Establishes a causal contribution, not a complete motion-perception algorithm.

### 27. Self et al. (2012)

[Different glutamate receptors convey feedforward and recurrent processing in macaque V1](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1119527109). DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1119527109.

**Evidence type:** Macaque V1 pharmacology and recordings.

**Supports:** Initial visually driven responses and later figure-ground modulation had different receptor dependence.

**Boundary:** Specific recurrent modulation, not proof that every feedback projection transmits a prediction.

### 28. Moore and Armstrong (2003)

[Selective gating of visual signals by microstimulation of frontal cortex](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature01341). DOI: 10.1038/nature01341.

**Evidence type:** Macaque subthreshold FEF stimulation and V4 recording.

**Supports:** Frontal stimulation enhanced spatially corresponding visual responses without requiring an eye movement.

**Boundary:** Supports a causal control-to-content link; a literal digital attention mask is an implementation choice.

### 29. Reynolds, Chelazzi and Desimone (1999)

[Competitive mechanisms subserve attention in macaque areas V2 and V4](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.19-05-01736.1999). DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.19-05-01736.1999.

**Evidence type:** Macaque visual recordings with paired stimuli and attention.

**Supports:** Attention biased responses toward those elicited by the attended stimulus alone.

**Boundary:** Competition is task- and population-dependent; not a universal winner-take-all circuit.

### 30. Goodale et al. (1991)

[A neurological dissociation between perceiving objects and grasping them](https://doi.org/10.1038/349154a0). DOI: 10.1038/349154a0.

**Evidence type:** Human neuropsychology.

**Supports:** Visual form recognition and aspects of visually guided grasping dissociated.

**Boundary:** Patient evidence does not mean ventral and dorsal streams never interact.

### 31. Borra et al. (2008 (online 2007))

[Cortical Connections of the Macaque Anterior Intraparietal (AIP) Area](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhm146). DOI: 10.1093/cercor/bhm146.

**Evidence type:** Macaque anatomical tracing.

**Supports:** AIP connects with inferior parietal, somatosensory, inferotemporal, premotor F5 and prefrontal territories.

**Boundary:** Wiring supports convergence for grasp-related computation; exact coordinate transformations need additional functional evidence.

### 32. Padberg et al. (2009)

[Thalamocortical connections of parietal somatosensory cortical fields in macaque monkeys are highly divergent and convergent](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhn229). DOI: 10.1093/cercor/bhn229.

**Evidence type:** Macaque anatomical tracing.

**Supports:** Multiple thalamic sources reach multiple somatosensory fields through convergent and divergent connections.

**Boundary:** Do not replace the actual parallel organization with a strict area-3b-to-1-to-2 chain.

### 33. Romanski et al. (1999)

[Dual streams of auditory afferents target multiple domains in the primate prefrontal cortex](https://doi.org/10.1038/16056). DOI: 10.1038/16056.

**Evidence type:** Primate anatomical tracing.

**Supports:** Anterior and caudal auditory association pathways target different prefrontal domains.

**Boundary:** Identity and spatial/action interpretations combine anatomy with task evidence; they are not labels carried by every axon.

### 34. Mesgarani and Chang (2012)

[Selective cortical representation of attended speaker in multi-talker speech perception](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11020). DOI: 10.1038/nature11020.

**Evidence type:** Human intracranial recordings.

**Supports:** Nonprimary auditory cortical representations preferentially reflected the attended speaker.

**Boundary:** Attention affects representation; this alone does not identify where all speech meanings are computed.

### 35. Kording et al. (2007)

[Causal Inference in Multisensory Perception](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0000943). DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0000943.

**Evidence type:** Human audiovisual psychophysics and model comparison.

**Supports:** Behavior is explained by considering whether sensory signals share a cause before combining estimates.

**Boundary:** The fitted Bayesian model is an account of behavior, not an identified cellular implementation.

### 36. Rohe and Noppeney (2015)

[Cortical Hierarchies Perform Bayesian Causal Inference in Multisensory Perception](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002073). DOI: 10.1371/journal.pbio.1002073.

**Evidence type:** Human audiovisual fMRI and computational model comparison.

**Supports:** Representations along a cortical hierarchy are consistent with different stages of causal inference and combination.

**Boundary:** Correlational model comparison does not establish an exact universal Bayesian algorithm.

### 37. Ernst and Banks (2002)

## Verification boundaries

The approximate one-second auditory persistence description is tied to the cited experiment; an unverified universal four-second figure was excluded. Working-memory capacity estimates are stimulus- and task-specific. The 2026 macaque maintenance study appeared online in December 2025. A newly indexed September 2026 olfactory-learning paper could not be inspected and was not used. No claims depend on that inaccessible paper.

The everyday activities are mechanism-constrained reconstructions. Their exact ordering, information fields and neural-network module boundaries are proposed by the synthesis, not attributed to a single whole-brain recording study.
